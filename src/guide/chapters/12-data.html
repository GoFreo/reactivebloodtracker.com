Your data, privacy and backups

Your readings, meals and notes stay on this device unless you export them or switch on an option that shares them. There is no account to create. Nothing is shared unless you ask. The app has no advertising and no analytics code.

Because your records live in your browser, clearing your browser's data for this site deletes them. Export a backup now and then (Home → Export), and on an iPhone, add the app to your Home Screen (see below).

Go deeper: exactly what leaves your phone, and when
  • Barcode lookups. When a barcode isn't in My foods or your food bank, its digits are sent to Open Food Facts to find the product.
  • Household food bank (optional, needs the sync passcode). Only product facts you save: barcode, name, carbohydrate and serving size. Never your meals.
  • Parse with AI. Only when you tap it. The words you typed, and the photo if you added one, are sent through this app's server to an AI service to estimate carbohydrate. Don't include anything in a food description that you wouldn't want sent.
  • Libre sync. When you tap Sync, this app's server asks Abbott's LibreLinkUp for your latest readings and passes them straight to your phone. It doesn't keep them.
  • Dexcom. You sign in on Dexcom's own page. The sign-in token is kept on your phone, and the app's server passes your readings straight through without keeping them.
  • Keep my devices in sync (Settings, off by default). If you switch it on, your glucose, food and diary records are also stored on the app's server so your phone and computer can share them. The host encrypts stored data, but it is not end-to-end encrypted, so only switch it on if you are comfortable with that. Food photos always stay on the device.
  • Like any website, the host sees ordinary web requests when the app loads.
Go deeper: how stored data can disappear, and what to do

Web browsers store a site's data in a place they are allowed to clear. By default it lasts as long as there is space and you don't delete it, but a browser can clear it when the device is short of space, and when it does it clears everything for that site at once, never part of it.[[mdn-storage]] A site you visit regularly is very unlikely to be cleared.[[mdn-storage]]

Safari is stricter. It deletes a website's stored data after seven days of Safari use without you interacting with that site. A web app added to the Home Screen is not part of Safari and has its own counter of days of use.[[webkit-itp]] So on an iPhone or iPad, open Share, choose Add to Home Screen, and open the app from there.

Go deeper: backups, a new phone, and sharing
  • Back up from Home → Export: download a CSV of your readings, meals and notes. Restore or move to another device with the Import box on the same screen.
  • My foods has its own backup button (a small file you can restore on any device).
  • Sharing with your doctor or family happens only when you decide: print the report or send a file. The app never sends anything by itself.
