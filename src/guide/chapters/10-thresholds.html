Warning levels: your low and high lines

In Settings → Glucose warning thresholds you set your own low and high lines. Readings are coloured against them, and the graph draws them as dashed lines with shading where glucose crossed them. They are left blank until you choose, because this app never picks your numbers for you.

One rule: the low line can't be set below 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL). You can set it higher for extra margin.

Go deeper: why 3.9 is the floor

3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) is the "alert value" in the American Diabetes Association's three levels of low glucose.[[ada-2026-s6]] The app refuses to let your low line sit below it, so a setting can't hide a reading that doctors treat as a warning.

A floor is not advice. It is one fixed, published number that only stops a less safe setting. The app doesn't recommend what number you choose, and it doesn't suggest what to do about a low.

Go deeper: why you might set it higher

A higher low line warns you sooner. Australian first-aid pages already draw the line at 4.0 mmol/L,[[diabetesaus-hypo,healthdirect-hypo]] and some people add their own margin for particular situations, the same way you might drive to a lower speed limit than the sign. The right number for you is a decision for you and your doctors.

Go deeper: what the colours and tones mean, and what they are not
  • Colours. The latest-reading card and the finger-prick diamonds are red when a reading is below your low line, amber when it is above your high line, and green when it is between them. The graph also shades the stretches below your low line. Tap "why" on the latest-reading card for the reason in words.
  • A tone plays when a low or high reading appears while the app is open (you can turn sounds off in Settings).
  • It is not an alarm. This app can't wake your phone or sound when it is closed or the phone is locked. Keep using your device's own alarms.
