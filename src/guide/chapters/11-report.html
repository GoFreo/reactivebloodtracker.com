Reports for your doctor, and backups

Home → Export turns your records into something you can take to an appointment or keep as a backup. Pick the dates and the screen shows what is in them before you make anything. Then make a report to print or save as a PDF, and, if you want one, a spreadsheet file (CSV). Nothing is sent anywhere. The files are made on your phone, and you choose what to do with them.

On a computer, the same button opens the print window. Choose Save as PDF as the printer. Where your browser can share a file, a Share… button appears beside Download CSV and sends the file through your phone's own share sheet.

Go deeper: what the report holds, and how each part is worked out

After the first page the report continues with these sections:

  • Low spells. Every stretch where the sensor read below your low line: when it started, the lowest reading, how long it lasted, and what was logged around it. That is up to three meals from the 5 hours before (nearest first, so a snack after lunch shows both), any notes from 90 minutes before to 30 minutes after, and a finger-prick from the half hour either side.
  • Meals and what followed. Each meal with the reading before it and the highest and lowest in the next 5 hours, the same figures as the Meals view.
  • Finger-pricks and the sensor. Each finger-prick beside what each sensor read at that moment, with the average difference and how often they were close.
  • Notes and symptoms. Every diary note, with its time.
  • Every entry. The complete list, for anyone who wants to check a number.
  • How to read this report. A short note on how sensors and finger-pricks differ, and what the report is and isn't.

The averages and shares use one sensor's readings (the one with the most in your dates), so two sensors worn together are never blended. They count readings, not minutes. Variation is shown as a standard deviation and as a percentage of the average.

Go deeper: why the low spells are laid out this way

A doctor looking into low glucose will usually want three things documented together: symptoms, a low glucose measured at the time, and relief when glucose is raised.[[endo-2009]] The low-spell table puts those side by side: the sensor's low, the note you wrote around it, the finger-prick that checked it, and the meal before it. The report doesn't decide whether anything is wrong. It gives your doctor the pieces in one place.

Go deeper: the spreadsheet (CSV) and backups
  • The columns. Type, When, Detail, Timestamp, Value, Unit, Note, Text, Source and Device. The first three are the readable part. The rest let the file be restored exactly. Tap "What's in each column" on the Export screen for a plain-English guide.
  • Source and Device say where each reading came from. They mean a restored file keeps your sensor readings as sensor readings. A file made before they existed still imports, but its readings come back as typed-in ones.
  • Restoring. Use the Import box at the bottom of the Export screen. Entries already there are skipped, so importing the same file twice never doubles anything.
  • What a backup doesn't hold. A CSV has readings, meals (as their text) and notes. It doesn't hold food photos, saved meals, your devices or your settings. My foods has its own backup button.

Back up now and then. Your records live in your browser, and clearing the browser's data deletes them.
