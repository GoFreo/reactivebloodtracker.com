Reading the graph and the Meals view

Readings → Graph draws your glucose over time. The main line is your sensor's smoothed average, which calms sensor noise so the shape is easy to see. Grey dots are the raw readings, diamonds are finger-pricks, triangles along the bottom are meals, and pink shading marks stretches below your low line.

The graph shows what happened. It doesn't say whether a number is good or bad. A sensor also runs a few minutes behind a finger-prick, so the two won't always match, especially during a fast rise or fall (see the chapter on sensors and finger-pricks).

Go deeper: how the app chooses what to mark
  • The smoothed line is a centred 30-minute average, worked out separately for each stretch of continuous data. It never draws across a gap, so it can't invent readings where the sensor had none.
  • Peaks and troughs are labelled only when they stand out by at least 1.5 mmol/L from the readings around them, so small wobbles aren't labelled.
  • Shaded low and high spells come from the raw readings, not the smoothed line, so a short low still shows. Finger-pricks aren't counted in a spell. They are too sparse to say how long anything lasted, so each one has its own diamond.
  • Grey "No data" bands show where the sensor had no readings.

None of this is a diagnosis. It marks what the numbers did so you can look.

Go deeper: time ranges, units and the sideways view

The buttons above the graph switch between 2 hours, 4, 6, 12, 24 hours and 7 days. Turn your phone sideways and Readings shows the graph on the left and the list on the right, so you can match a reading to the curve.

Glucose can be shown in mmol/L or mg/dL; choose under Settings, Unit. The two differ by a factor of about 18: for example 3.9 mmol/L is 70 mg/dL, and 3.0 mmol/L is 54 mg/dL.[[ada-2026-s6]]

Go deeper: the Meals view, five hours after each meal

The window is five hours because low sugar after a meal is described as arriving within about four hours of eating, and often two to four.[[mayo-reactive,cleveland-reactive,nhs-northtees]] The app uses sensor readings when there are enough of them, and finger-pricks otherwise.

If you eat again within those five hours, the numbers include that meal's effect too. The card reports the facts from your own data. It is not a verdict on the meal.
