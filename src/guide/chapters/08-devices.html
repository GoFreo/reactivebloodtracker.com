Your devices: fitting, wearing and using them well

Each maker publishes instructions for fitting and using its device, and those instructions come first. The notes below gather the main points from each maker's own pages in one place, for the three devices this app works with: the FreeStyle Libre 2 Plus sensor, the Dexcom ONE+ sensor and the Accu-Chek Guide Me meter. If the leaflet in your box says something different, follow the leaflet. Instructions can differ by country and model.

Three habits help with any sensor: prepare the skin the way the maker says, change the site each time, and have your next sensor ready before the current one ends.[[abbott-qrg,dexcom-insert]]

The app records each Libre sensor's start date for you when you sync. If a Libre sensor stops working before the end of its wear time, Abbott says to contact its customer service.[[abbott-qrg]]

Go deeper: FreeStyle Libre 2 Plus

At a glance. It can be worn for up to 15 days, and needs a one-hour warm-up after it is applied before it gives readings.[[abbott-qrg,abbott-au-faq]]

Fitting it

  • Back of the upper arm only. Abbott says that in other places the sensor may not work properly.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Pick the spot. Choose skin that generally stays flat in daily life, with no bending or folding. Avoid scars, moles, stretch marks and lumps, and stay at least 2.5 cm (1 inch) from an insulin injection site.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Prepare the skin. Wash with plain soap, dry, then clean with an alcohol wipe and let it air-dry. This removes oily residue that can stop the sensor sticking. Clean hands before you handle the sensor.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Check the codes. The sensor pack and applicator have the same sensor code. Check they match before use.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Change the site each time, to prevent discomfort or skin irritation.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Don't use a kit that looks damaged or already opened, or that is past its expiry date.[[abbott-qrg]]

Wearing it

  • Skin reaction. If the skin around or under the sensor becomes significantly irritated, Abbott says to remove it, stop using the system, and speak to your health professional before continuing.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Exercise and sweat can loosen a sensor. If it comes loose or its tip comes out of the skin, you may get no readings or unreliable low readings. Replace it, don't reinsert it, and contact Abbott.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Scans and treatments. Remove the sensor before an MRI, a CT scan or diathermy treatment, and before an X-ray.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Storage before use. Keep the sensor kit between about 2 and 28 °C (36 and 82 °F) and at 10 to 90% humidity. If it may get hotter, refrigerate it, but don't freeze it. Never leave it in a parked car on a hot day.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • One sensor, one person, one use. Don't reuse a sensor, and don't share one.[[abbott-qrg]]

Using it

  • Keep a finger-prick method available. Abbott says that if your sensor stops working and you have no spare, you must use another way to measure your glucose.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Look after the phone. After a phone update, open the Libre app and check its settings. On an iPhone, Screen Time can stop alarms unless the Libre app is on its Always Allowed list.[[abbott-qrg]]
  • Check your supplements. More than 1000 mg of vitamin C a day can falsely raise readings, and aspirin can slightly lower them.[[abbott-qrg,abbott-substances]]

How this app connects

Through LibreLinkUp, Abbott's sharing service. In Abbott's own app you share your readings, then this app asks for the latest ones when you tap Sync, using the sync passcode saved in Settings. Abbott says LibreLinkUp is not intended for treatment decisions.[[abbott-au-faq]] This app is independent of Abbott and isn't endorsed by it.

Go deeper: Dexcom ONE+

At a glance. It can be worn for up to 10 days, plus a 12-hour grace period at the end, and has a 30-minute warm-up.[[dexcom-au-launch]] It sends a reading every 5 minutes to the Dexcom app or to a receiver.[[dexcom-setup]] A sensor can't be restarted once it has finished.[[dexcom-insert]]

Fitting it

  • Where. From age 7, on the abdomen or the back of the upper arm. Children aged 2 to 6 can also use the upper buttocks. Dexcom says it hasn't tested or approved other sites.[[dexcom-au-sites,dexcom-insert]]
  • Choose well. Look for a place with some padding. Avoid bony areas with little fatty tissue, and skin that is scarred, irritated or tattooed, or where clothing rubs.[[dexcom-au-sites,dexcom-insert]]
  • Stay clear of a pump or injection site by at least 8 cm.[[dexcom-insert]]
  • Rotate sites with each new sensor, to let the skin heal and reduce irritation.[[dexcom-insert]]
  • Pair it using the pairing code on the applicator (it has a QR code). The app or receiver walks you through the steps. An overpatch is available to help keep the sensor in place.[[dexcom-setup]]

Using it

  • Calibration is optional. Dexcom says its systems are designed to work without it. It may help if readings differ from finger-pricks by 20 to 30% for several hours. If you do calibrate, use a finger-prick from a blood glucose meter (never an alternate-site reading), with clean, dry hands, and enter it within 5 minutes, only when your glucose is steady, in one display device only, and never the Dexcom's own number.[[dexcom-au-calibrate,dexcom-au-mismatch]] Roche says the same from the meter's side: don't use blood from the palm or upper arm to calibrate a continuous monitor.[[roche-guideme]]
  • When readings and symptoms disagree, Dexcom says to treat using the number that best fits your symptoms if the sensor and the meter are within its tolerance, and to use the meter if they aren't.[[dexcom-au-mismatch]]
  • Before you update your phone, check Dexcom's compatibility chart. It can take Dexcom some time to finish testing the newest versions of iOS and Android.[[dexcom-compat]]

How this app connects

You sign in on Dexcom's own page, not inside this app. Dexcom holds back data for other apps by one hour in the United States and three hours elsewhere,[[dexcom-api-delay]] so the Dexcom line here can be up to three hours behind the Dexcom app. Settings → Dexcom ONE+ shows whether the connection is live for you. This app is independent of Dexcom and isn't endorsed by it.

Go deeper: Accu-Chek Guide Me meter

This is Roche's US manual, so check the leaflet in your own box too.[[roche-guideme]] The kit uses Accu-Chek Guide test strips and a Softclix lancing device, and the meter stores 720 results with their times and dates.[[roche-guideme]]

A finger-prick, step by step

  1. Wash your hands with warm soapy water and dry them well. Dirty or wet hands could affect the result.
  2. Check the Use By date on the strip container. Don't use strips past it. Take out one strip and close the cap tightly straight away.
  3. Put the metal end of the strip into the meter. It turns on.
  4. When the flashing drop appears, press the lancing device firmly against the side of your fingertip and release it.
  5. Gently squeeze to get a drop of blood. Touch the yellow edge of the strip to the drop. Don't put blood on top of the strip.
  6. Take your finger away when the meter signals, and read the result. Remove and throw away the strip, and discard the lancet safely in a puncture-proof container.
  7. Wash and dry your hands again.[[roche-guideme]]

Strips and the control test

  • Keep strips in their original container with the cap tight. Moisture can damage them and give wrong results.[[roche-guideme]]
  • Do a control test when you open a new box of strips, or when you think a result is wrong. Throw away control solution 3 months after opening, or once it is past its Use By date.[[roche-guideme]]
  • If a result appears before you have applied any blood, don't act on it.[[roche-guideme]]

Palm and upper-arm testing

The meter allows blood from the palm or upper arm, but only at steady times, when glucose isn't changing quickly, because glucose changes faster in the fingertip. It should never be used to calibrate a continuous monitor or to work out insulin doses. Blood from the fingertip can be used at any time.[[roche-guideme]] Because a drop after a meal is a fast change, Roche's rule points to the fingertip for those checks.

Limits and cautions

  • Not for diagnosing or screening for diabetes.[[roche-guideme]]
  • Not for use if your hematocrit (the proportion of red cells in your blood) is above 65% or below 10%, or in severe shock, dehydration or critical illness. Very high vitamin C can give wrong results. The meter hasn't been tested above 10,150 feet (about 3,100 metres).[[roche-guideme]]
  • Roche says not to change your treatment based on a result that doesn't match how you feel, and to keep a back-up way to test.[[roche-guideme]]
  • Never share the meter or the lancing device, not even with family. It risks passing on infection.[[roche-guideme]]

Bluetooth

A meter can pair with only one device at a time, and pairing with a second device replaces the first. Keep the two within about a metre. The 6-digit PIN is on the back of the meter, and the meter gives up 30 seconds after asking for it. Once paired and Bluetooth is on, results are sent to the paired device automatically, and turning Bluetooth off doesn't unpair it.[[roche-guideme]]

How this app connects

The app reads your meter's stored results over Bluetooth and keeps each one once, so syncing twice never doubles a reading. It does this through your browser's Web Bluetooth feature, which Chrome, Edge, Opera and Samsung Internet have, but Firefox and Safari, including on iPhone, do not.[[mdn-bcd-bluetooth,mdn-bluetooth]] In practice, sync the meter from Chrome on a Mac, a Windows PC or an Android phone. On an iPhone, type the finger-prick in. Switch the meter on just before you tap Sync, because its Bluetooth only stays on for a short time. This app is independent of Roche and isn't endorsed by it.

Go deeper: wearing two sensors at once

Two sensors from different makers will rarely agree exactly, and both can be right within their own tolerance.[[dexcom-au-mismatch,abbott-au-differences]] If you wear both, expect a gap between the lines, check the Compare view, and use finger-pricks as the tie-breaker. Each maker's own fitting rules apply to its sensor.
