If you feel low right now

If you feel low, treat it and check with a finger-prick. Don't wait for the app. In an emergency, call 000.

Australian guidance describes a low as a reading below 4.0 mmol/L.[[diabetesaus-hypo,healthdirect-hypo]] For people on insulin or diabetes tablets, the usual first step is about 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate, such as 6 to 7 jellybeans or 150 to 200 ml of fruit juice, then re-checking after 10 to 15 minutes.[[diabetesaus-hypo,healthdirect-hypo]] If someone is unconscious or can't swallow, don't give them food or drink, and call 000. Call 000 as well if they are drowsy, or if you can't treat the low yourself.[[diabetesaus-hypo,healthdirect-hypo]]

That guidance was written for lows in people with diabetes. It doesn't cover every situation, including lows that follow meals in people without diabetes. Ask your care team what plan suits you, and follow it before anything in this guide.

Go deeper: what counts as a "low"? Three levels

Doctors group lows into three levels.[[ada-2026-s6]]

  • Level 1: below 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) but not below 3.0 mmol/L (54 mg/dL). 3.9 is treated as the "alert value".
  • Level 2: below 3.0 mmol/L (54 mg/dL). The American Diabetes Association describes this as the point where symptoms of the brain not getting enough glucose begin, and it needs immediate action.
  • Level 3: a severe event, where a person's mind or body is affected enough that they need someone else's help, whatever the number is.

The International Hypoglycaemia Study Group asked for results below 3.0 mmol/L to be reported separately because they are clinically significant, not just a warning.[[ihsg-2017]] Australian first-aid pages draw the line at 4.0 mmol/L.[[diabetesaus-hypo,healthdirect-hypo]] This app never lets your low warning go below 3.9 mmol/L, and you can set it higher (see the chapter on warning levels).

These levels were written mainly for people with diabetes. They are not a diagnosis for anyone.

Go deeper: how a low can feel

People describe shakiness, dizziness or light-headedness, sweating, hunger, a fast or uneven heartbeat, feeling weak or tired, feeling irritable or anxious, headache and confusion.[[mayo-reactive]] Other pages add tingling lips, tongue or cheeks and blurred vision, and, in a severe low, slurred speech, strange behaviour, seizures or unconsciousness.[[healthdirect-hypo]]

A low can happen asleep. Signs include crying out or nightmares, sheets or clothes soaked in sweat, and waking tired, irritable or confused.[[niddk-hypo]]

Some people don't feel the warning signs at all. This is called hypoglycaemia unawareness, and it can follow repeated lows.[[niddk-hypo,ada-2026-s6]] It is one reason a sensor can help, and one reason to talk to your doctor if lows keep happening.

Go deeper: when to see a doctor

healthdirect says to see a doctor if lows are frequent or severe.[[healthdirect-hypo]] An NHS hospital trust's page on reactive hypoglycaemia advises a blood glucose test when symptoms occur.[[nhs-northtees]]

Don't ignore symptoms that don't match a reading. Abbott's guide for the Libre 2 says that if your symptoms aren't consistent with your glucose readings, you should consult your health professional.[[abbott-qrg]]

A doctor looking into low glucose will usually want three things documented together: your symptoms, a low glucose measured at the time, and relief once glucose is raised.[[endo-2009]] A time-stamped record of readings, meals and how you felt is exactly that. It is why this app has a report you can print for your appointment.
