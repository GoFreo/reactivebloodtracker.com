Glossary
Alert value
3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL). Below it counts as a level 1 low in the American Diabetes Association's scheme.[[ada-2026-s6]]
Calibration
Telling a sensor what a finger-prick measured so it can adjust. Optional on a Dexcom sensor.[[dexcom-au-calibrate]] The Libre 2 is described as factory-calibrated.[[libre2-women]]
Carbohydrate (carbs)
Sugars and starches. The "carbohydrate" figure on an Australian food label includes both.[[fsanz-nip]]
CGM (continuous glucose monitor)
A sensor worn on the body that measures glucose in the fluid under the skin every few minutes. The Libre and the Dexcom are CGMs.[[abbott-qrg,dexcom-setup]]
Compression low
A falsely low sensor reading caused by pressure on the sensor, often while you sleep.[[ada-2026-s6]]
Control solution
A test liquid with a known glucose level, used to check that a meter and its strips are working together.[[roche-guideme]]
Finger-prick (or blood glucose meter test)
A glucose measurement from a drop of blood. It is the reference the graph's diamonds show.
Glycaemic index (GI)
A ranking of carbohydrate foods by how much they raise blood glucose after eating.[[sydney-gi]]
Hematocrit
The proportion of your blood that is red cells. The Accu-Chek Guide Me isn't for use above 65% or below 10%.[[roche-guideme]]
Hypoglycaemia (a "hypo", low blood sugar)
Glucose that is too low. Australian guidance puts the line below 4.0 mmol/L.[[diabetesaus-hypo,healthdirect-hypo]]
Hypoglycaemia unawareness
Not noticing the warning signs of a low until glucose is dangerously low. It can follow repeated lows.[[niddk-hypo]]
Interstitial fluid
The thin layer of fluid between the cells under the skin. Sensors measure glucose here, not in blood.[[abbott-au-differences]]
Lag
The delay before a change in blood glucose shows up in the interstitial fluid. Abbott puts it at 5 to 10 minutes.[[abbott-au-differences]]
Levels 1, 2 and 3
Three grades of low: below 3.9 mmol/L, below 3.0 mmol/L, and a severe event needing someone else's help.[[ada-2026-s6]]
LibreLinkUp
Abbott's sharing service, which lets family or carers (and this app) see a Libre user's readings. It is not intended for treatment decisions.[[abbott-au-faq]]
MARD
Mean absolute relative difference: on average, how far a sensor's readings are from a reference, as a percentage. Lower is closer.[[libre2-men]]
mmol/L and mg/dL
Two ways to write glucose. Multiply mmol/L by about 18 to get mg/dL. For example 3.9 mmol/L is 70 mg/dL.[[ada-2026-s6]]
Postprandial
After a meal. Reactive hypoglycaemia is sometimes called postprandial hypoglycaemia.[[mayo-reactive]]
Reactive hypoglycaemia
Low blood sugar that happens after a meal, usually within about four hours.[[mayo-reactive]]
Time in range
The share of time glucose spends inside a target band. For people with diabetes, experts suggest 3.9 to 10.0 mmol/L.[[tir-2019]]
Warm-up
The time after a sensor is applied before it gives readings: one hour for the Libre 2 Plus, 30 minutes for the Dexcom ONE+.[[abbott-au-faq,dexcom-au-launch]]
Whipple's triad
Three things doctors look for together: symptoms, a low glucose measured at the time, and relief when glucose is raised.[[endo-2009]]
