**Subject:** Glucose and food records for [Patient name], shared at their request

Dear [Doctor name],

[Patient name] has asked to share their glucose and food records with you. They keep them in Reactive Blood
Tracker, an app that records glucose readings, meals and symptoms in one place. They track it for
[condition, e.g. "reactive hypoglycaemia after weight-loss surgery"].

**What the app does.** It records finger-prick readings (typed in or synced from the meter) and, where the
patient uses one, readings from a continuous glucose sensor (FreeStyle Libre 2 Plus). It links each reading
to what they ate, with carbohydrate figures taken from food packaging, and to notes such as "felt shaky". It
shows what happened. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment, or suggest doses or portion sizes.

**Why they're sharing it.** To give you a clearer picture between appointments: when lows or highs happen,
how they relate to meals, and how often they happen. Whether and how to use it is entirely your call. If you'd
rather not receive these, a reply saying so is enough, and the patient can stop sharing at any time.

**Reading the report and graph**

- The **line** is the sensor's glucose, smoothed so the overall trend is easy to follow.
- **Peaks** are marked above the line and **troughs** below it, each with its value and time.
- **Shaded bands** show time spent below the patient's low threshold ([low], in mmol/L) or above their high
  threshold ([high]). Each crossing is listed with its time and duration.
- **Diamonds** are finger-prick readings. Sensor and finger-prick values can differ: the sensor reads tissue
  fluid, which lags blood by about 5–15 minutes and is less accurate during fast changes and in the low range.
- **Meal markers** along the bottom show when food was logged; tapping one shows the meal and its carbohydrates.
- The low threshold can't be set below 3.9 mmol/L (the ADA "level 1" hypoglycaemia threshold).

**Please note.** The data is entered and synced by the patient, and devices and food labels can be wrong. It's
a record to support your assessment, not a clinical measurement.

With thanks,
[Patient name]
