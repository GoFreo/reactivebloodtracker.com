Your setup

Please read: what this app is, and isn't

This app helps you record and understand your glucose, food and how they relate, and helps the people around you keep an eye out. It shows what happened. It does not diagnose, treat, or tell you what or how much to eat, and it never suggests medication or insulin doses.

  • Readings, carb figures and patterns can be wrong. Treat them as a guide, not the final word.
  • Always follow the advice of your doctor, diabetes educator or dietitian over anything shown here.
  • If you feel low, treat it and check with a finger-prick. Don't wait for the app.
  • In an emergency call 000.

Why the Libre and a finger-prick disagree

They measure different things. A finger-prick measures glucose in your blood. The Libre sensor measures it in the fluid under your skin, which trails the blood by about 5 to 15 minutes. When your glucose is moving fast, such as after a meal or during a crash, the two can be well apart. Sensors also read less accurately in the low range, on the first day of a new sensor, and when pressed on while you sleep. Published studies put the Libre 2's average difference from lab tests at about 9 to 13%, and higher in some groups and during lows. That's why the graph shows both, and why a finger-prick is the one to trust before acting on a low.

If you think you're low

Australian guidance (Diabetes Australia, healthdirect) describes a low as below 4.0 mmol/L and a first step of about 15 g of fast-acting carbohydrate, such as 6 to 7 jellybeans or 150 to 200 ml of fruit juice, then re-checking after 15 minutes. That guidance is written mainly for people on insulin or diabetes tablets. Your own treatment plan from your care team comes first. Reactive and post-surgery lows can behave differently.

How the parts work

Glucose
Type a reading, sync your Accu-Chek meter over Bluetooth (Chrome only), or sync your Libre with the passcode in Settings.
Food and the meal builder
Describe what you ate, or build it from ingredients: grams × the pack's "per 100 g" carbs. Portion buttons use the pack's serving size. Totals only add figures you or the pack supplied.
Barcodes and My foods
A scan checks your saved foods, then your household food bank, then the public Open Food Facts database. Public data can be wrong: check it against the pack before saving.
"Why did your sugar spike?"
When the Libre shows a fast rise or drop with nothing logged, the app asks what happened. It's a prompt to record, not a diagnosis.
Readings → Meals
Each meal with what your glucose did over the next 5 hours. These are facts from your own data, not advice about what to eat.
Export
A spreadsheet file or a printable summary for your doctor, only when you choose to make one.

Your data

Your readings, meals and notes stay on this device unless you export them. Only these leave it: product facts you save to your household food bank (barcode, name, carbs, serving size, never your meals), food descriptions or photos you choose to send for AI parsing, and the Libre sync request. Clearing your browser's data for this site deletes what's stored here, so export or back up regularly.

Everyday eating

  • Nothing is strictly off-limits — the combination matters more than any single food.
  • Known trigger: a big hit of simple/refined carbs on their own — white bread, white pasta, white rice, pastries, sugary drinks, juice, lollies — especially on an empty stomach.
  • Steadier pattern: pair complex carbs with protein, fat, or fibre — wholegrains, legumes, nuts and seeds, vegetables — to soften the rise so there's less to crash from.
  • Smaller, more frequent meals (roughly every 3 hours) tend to work better than a few large ones.

Alcohol

  • Not all off the table, but there's a real mechanism at play: alcohol makes your liver prioritise breaking it down over topping up blood sugar, so it can trigger or worsen a crash on its own.
  • One study found a specific higher-risk combination: alcohol with a sugary mixer (their example was gin and tonic) — a starch-based drink like beer didn't provoke the same drop.
  • Risk increases alongside other factors — age, other health conditions, exercise around the same time.
  • General takeaway: drink with food rather than on an empty stomach, and be more cautious with sweet mixers and cocktails than plainer drinks.

Eating out & social occasions

  • Check the menu ahead of time so you're not deciding under pressure at the table.
  • Grilled, baked, or steamed on a balanced plate tends to beat fried or sugar-heavy dishes — most places will happily swap a side.
  • Don't arrive starving — skipping a meal before a dinner out is a common setup for overeating simple carbs and crashing afterward.

Glucose warning thresholds

Left blank on purpose — nothing gets colored until you set your own numbers here. For reference while you're deciding (confirm with your doctor, this app isn't recommending a value): the ADA defines hypoglycemia as below 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL).

You can set this higher than 3.9 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) for extra personal margin — same idea as driving to your own lower speed limit — but this app won't let it go lower than that standard alert point.

My devices

Each physical meter or sensor you've used, with its serial number — so a sync can tell "this is your saved meter" from "this is a different one nearby," and so a new Libre sensor's start date is on record automatically when you sync, for Abbott replacement claims.

Glucose source

Use the Sync devices button on Home to connect — the list that opens shows every nearby Bluetooth device, not just meters, so look for one named something like "Accu-Chek" or "Guide Me" and pick that one. If nothing shows up at all, make sure Bluetooth is turned on for this browser in your Mac's System Settings → Privacy & Security → Bluetooth, and that the meter is actually still in its pairing/searching mode (some meters only stay discoverable for a short window after you trigger it).

Libre 2 Plus (CGM) sync

Saving your passcode here means Sync devices on Home can pull your Libre readings without asking for it each time. Pulls roughly the last 12 hours; readings already saved are skipped, so syncing often is fine. The passcode stops anyone else who finds this site from reading your glucose.

Dexcom ONE+ (CGM)

Connecting takes you to Dexcom's own sign-in page. You never type your Dexcom password into this app, and you can withdraw access from your Dexcom account at any time. Once connected, Sync devices on Home pulls new readings. Dexcom holds readings back by about 3 hours outside the US (1 hour in the US), so the newest ones arrive later than in Dexcom's own app.

Unexplained rise & drop questions

Uses your Libre (CGM) readings only, since finger-pricks are too far apart to show a rise. For drops the app looks back 4 hours, because a reactive low usually follows a meal by 2–4 hours. These are just triggers for a question. They aren't medical limits.

Reminders

Checked only while the app is open (in-app banner above). Background push notifications on an iPhone home-screen app are unreliable — this does not rely on them.

Plays while the app is open — a phone can't let a website override its own system notification sound, so this is a real, distinct tone from inside the app itself, not a substitute for it. Your own custom sound file is a fast-follow, not built yet.
