## Version B (recommended): 275 words, about two minutes with pauses

**1. Hook** (graph pushing in on the peak, then the crash)

After a meal, your blood sugar climbs. Then, an hour or two later, it *crashes*.
That crash is almost impossible to describe to a doctor from memory.
Reactive Blood Tracker puts it on the record.

**2. Log it** (Home screen; the glucose box, food box and Sync button highlighted in turn)

Log a reading, a meal, or a note in seconds. There's no account. Your records stay on your phone.

**3. It asks** (the "why did your sugar spike?" card; tap Yes; a typed answer)

People forget to log things. *Sensors don't.*
When your sensor shows a sudden rise or drop with nothing logged, the app simply asks: *what happened?*
Two chocolate biscuits? A whisky after the footy? Whatever the answer, it goes on the record.

**4. The graph** (Readings, Graph; zoom on the peak, the trough, a finger-prick diamond, a meal marker; then Meals)

Then the graph shows the whole story.
Here, toast and honey for breakfast sent glucose past nine. Two hours later, it fell below the low line.

And the Meals view shows what each meal did over the next five hours.

**5. Food** (typing, the barcode button, the meal builder, the portion buttons)

Logging food is quick. Type it, snap it, or scan the barcode, and the label's carbs fill in.
Build a meal from ingredients, then pick a portion: a quarter, a half, or a whole serve.

**6. Devices** (Sync tap; the device lights; My devices)

One Sync button brings in your FreeStyle Libre and your Accu-Chek meter, with Dexcom on the way.
All your devices, on one timeline.

**7. For your doctor** (Export; the printed report scrolling; the graph and table highlighted)

When it's time to see your doctor, print a clean report: the graph, every reading, every meal, for the dates you choose.
Nothing leaves your phone unless you send it.

**8. Close** (end card with the web address)

Reactive Blood Tracker shows you what happened. It never tells you what to eat, or how much insulin to take. That stays between you and your doctors.
Reactive Blood Tracker dot com.

## Version A (what the live video says today): 459 words

**Intro**
This is Reactive Blood Tracker.
It's an app for people whose blood sugar swings. It was built first for reactive hypoglycaemia, where a meal sends glucose up, and an hour or two later, it crashes.
That crash is hard to describe to a doctor from memory. This app puts it on the record.
Everything you'll see uses made-up demonstration data, not a real patient.

**1. Open it**
It opens from your phone's home screen, like any other app.
There's no account to create, and your records stay on your own phone, unless you choose to share them.
Home shows your latest reading, with quick ways to log a glucose reading, a meal, or a note.

**2. It asks**
People forget to log things. Sensors don't.
When the sensor shows a rise or a drop with nothing logged before it, the app asks what happened.
A couple of biscuits, a whisky after the footy, or just a sensor glitch. Whatever the answer, it goes on the record.

**3. See the pattern**
The graph is where it comes together.
Here's one day. Toast and honey for breakfast sent glucose up past nine. About two hours later, it fell below the low line.
Each peak and trough is labelled. Low spells are shaded, and listed underneath. Finger-prick checks show as diamonds, and meals sit as markers along the bottom.

**3b. Three days**
Over three days, the pattern is plain to see. Two crashes, both after carb-heavy breakfasts, and one calm day, after a smaller breakfast of yoghurt and berries.

**4. What each meal did**
The Meals view shows what each meal did over the next five hours: where glucose started, how high it went, and how low it fell afterwards.

**5. Logging food**
Logging food is quick. Type or say what you ate, photograph the plate, or scan a barcode, and the label's carbs fill in for you.
Build a meal from ingredients, and pick a portion: a quarter, a half, or a whole serve.
When it estimates from a photo, it says plainly when it isn't sure, instead of guessing.

**6. Your devices**
Readings come from the devices you already use.
One Sync button pulls in a FreeStyle Libre sensor, and an Accu-Chek meter over Bluetooth. Dexcom sensors connect through Dexcom's own sign-in, and that's being switched on now.
Each sensor is recorded with its start date, which helps when one fails and needs replacing.

**7. For your doctor**
When it's time to see the doctor, the app prints a clean report: the graph, and every reading, meal and note, for the dates you choose.
Nothing leaves your phone unless you decide to send it.

**Close**
Reactive Blood Tracker shows what happened. It never tells you what to eat, or how much insulin to take. That stays between you and your doctors.
Reactive Blood Tracker. At reactive blood tracker dot com.
